I constantly hear managers, tech leads, influencers, and even coworkers repeating the same phrase ad nauseam:

> "We no longer need to write code. AI writes code much better than we do."

For a few months, I genuinely believed that premise and kept an open mind, working on side projects to test the hypothesis. Early on, I felt an absurd increase in speed. Tasks were delivered faster, the product team was happy, and believed more tweaks and new features could keep coming to the team. I had the opportunity to build a project from scratch for a large audience, where the entire development team relied heavily on tools like Cursor, Claude Code, and Codex. As the project grew, a few things started to bother me.

No matter how hard I tried to take notes and understand the business rules, I sometimes found myself lost in the code, trying to figure out where the rule that managed a calculation lived, or where a variable was configured. I validated that feeling by talking to the team and noticed a pattern: nobody really knew the code well. In situations where we had to debug production issues, we were entirely dependent on AI to understand and locate parts of the codebase. It felt like we were no longer in control of the code. Over time, I realized that the bigger the project grew, the more the team's insecurity grew around implementing new features or doing refactors. The team was afraid of the code.

Another factor that bothered me was that all the productivity we gained at the start was lost. Tasks, debugging, and refinements started taking longer. Humans do not retain context the way AI does, and even though AI had produced the code, it often proved inefficient at answering questions about code it had written itself. In the end, I felt that although it is a powerful tool for writing documentation and tests, I was not convinced to delegate all of my work to AI. The core of the application, the domain, and the business rules need a fully human perspective. Maybe I still have not learned how to work at that stage with AI as a partner.

I feel there is pressure in the industry to be literally pushed toward "the new way of programming." To me, that looks more like a desperate attempt by an unsustainable market to create demand for a technology that has proven efficient, but not for everything it promises, nor at the volume and scale sold in so many ads. For building POCs, it is truly an incredible tool. Even so, I believe delegating the creation of all code to AI is a mistake in the long run.
